# Canon — the definitions every skill in this plugin shares

Load this when two concepts might be confused, when mapping research findings to a script structure, or when a skill needs a definition it does not own. Every model here is defined **once**, here. No skill redefines them.

**Section map, so a skill can cite the § it needs and a reader can grep straight to it.** §1 the three golden hook rules + the two pre-hook gates · §2 awareness stage → script flow · §3 the four-altitude hook stack · §4 the four visual types + the belief rule · §5 the glossary of colliding terms · §6 the iteration ladder + the six iteration variables · §7 what "winner" means (the two-lane metric rule) · §8 the non-negotiables. **Not needed for routine execution** — a skill that has inlined the rule it applies should not load this file at all.

Doctrine source: Nick Theriot, *The Art of Creating Ads That Scale* (2024), cited as `[T p.N]`. Verified against 2026 evidence — see `shared/evidence.md` for what changed and why.

## 1. The three golden hook rules — the pass/fail gate

Every hook must clear all three `[T p.32]`:

1. **Calls out the ideal audience**
2. **Implies a benefit**
3. **Drives curiosity**

This is the diagnostic, not a suggestion. It correctly explains all four of the book's spend-tier case studies `[T p.65–66]`: the $100 ad failed rules 2 and 3; the $1,000 and $10,000 ads passed all three and failed for reasons *outside* the hook; the $100,000 ad passed all three with a clear audience.

**Necessary but not sufficient.** Two gates sit outside the hook and must be checked first:

- **Is the desire proven and large?** "If it's not a proven desire across the marketplace or large enough, it will not scale" `[T p.65]`. The $1,000 ad had a perfect hook aimed at "romanticizing your life" — an unproven desire.
- **Is the audience unambiguous?** The $10,000 ad named "people who want a date night this weekend" and showed a woman alone — audience blur plus hook/visual mismatch `[T p.66]`.

Corollary rule: **calling out the audience is not enough on its own — give them a reason to pay attention** `[T p.33]`.

## 2. Awareness stage → script flow — the canonical mapping

Five awareness stages (the Schwartz lineage the book credits at `[T p.99]`) mapped onto the book's five script flows `[T p.47–49]`. **This mapping is the reason research findings can select a script structure instead of a writer guessing.**

| Awareness stage | Audience state | Script flow | The hook's job | Typical formats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem; feels only a symptom | **Symptom → Problem → Solution → Product → Offer** — "the most complex script you can write… its place when we're opening up unaware markets" `[T p.47]` | Name the symptom in their words | Expert explainer, story, native text |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the problem, doesn't know solution types exist | **Problem → Solution → Product → Offer** `[T p.48]` | Name the problem more sharply than they can | Problem visual, relatability, UGC |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows solution categories, comparing approaches | **Solution → Product → Offer** `[T p.48]` | Claim the solution and attach a mechanism | Demo, us-vs-them, mechanism reveal |
| **Product-aware** | Knows your product, not convinced; still shopping competitors | **Product → Offer** — focus on trust, superiority, objections `[T p.48]` | Lead with proof, not introduction | Testimonial, review, case study, founder |
| **Most-aware** | Ready; needs a reason to act now | **Offer** — rare, for major sales events `[T p.49]` | Urgency and terms | Offer card, countdown, stock callout |

**Selection rule:** the stage comes from research (what solutions have they already tried? `[T p.25, p.33]`), never from preference. Budget matters too — a small budget concentrated at one stage beats spread across five.

## 4. The four visual types — one model, used everywhere

Every photo ad **and every scene inside a video** is one of four `[T p.42, p.45]`:

1. **The benefit** — the desire achieved
2. **The problem** — the current state
3. **The product** — for proving a *better* mechanism, not a new one
4. **The product in action** — hardest, highest belief

**Ordering rule: text creates the visual, so always start with the text** `[T p.32]`. Then ask per line: *"How can I visually articulate this?"* `[T p.41, p.60]`

**Belief rule: people believe what they see, not what they hear** `[T p.40, p.77]`. This decides photo vs video — pick whichever *shows* what has to be believed.

## 5. Glossary — terms that collide, resolved

| Term | In this plugin it means | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|
| **Angle** (= messaging angle) | A core-truth statement at a pain × persona intersection | A hook tactic. A "curiosity angle" is a tactic, not an angle. |
| **Tactic** | The frame of a hook's claim (34 of them) | A mechanic (concept-level) or a format (production-level) |
| **Mechanic** | The cognitive move the whole concept makes | A tactic. *Contrarian* is a tactic that flips a belief in the opening line; *The Reframe* is a mechanic that builds to the flip as the point of the entire ad. |
| **Format** | The production vessel (testimonial, split screen, greenscreen…) | A mechanic. Format is the vessel; mechanic is the move inside it. |
| **Mechanism** | The *vehicle* that gets the customer from where they are to where they want to be `[T p.26]` | A mechanic. Different word, different layer — mechanism is positioning, mechanic is craft. |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Both a psychological trigger (hook layer) and a visual format (production layer) | Itself. Always qualify which layer. |
| **Iteration** | A **single-variable** change to a proven winner `[T p.85]` | A variation |
| **Variation** | A new hook and new concept around the **same core winning messaging** `[T p.86]` | An iteration or a new concept |
| **Concept** | New core messaging | A variation |
| **Hook rate** | Whatever the tool defines it as — check the denominator | A comparable cross-tool number. See `shared/evidence.md`. |

## 6. The iteration ladder — how far to push a winner

From `[T p.93]`, the rule that came out of a $200k/month account tanking after four months of repetitive messaging:

> **3 iterations → 3–6 variations → then move to new concepts.**

And the hard constraint: **"Do not just take the same video or photo and include a new hook in it… The same content over and over with just new hooks is a quick way to burn down an advertising campaign"** `[T p.86]`. Variations require new footage.

The six iteration variables `[T p.85–86]`: **environment, ethnicity, gender** (product dependent), **age, length, removal** ("can anything be removed to make it better?").

Escalation pattern `[T p.87]`: once messaging wins, broaden the demonstration and then push to *extreme* demonstrations — Flex Seal moved from a leaking window seal to drilling a hole in a boat and floating it.

## 7. What "winner" means — the two-lane metric rule

The book's strategic definition holds: **an ad wins if it lets the account spend more, profitably** `[T p.88]`. How that gets measured changed in 2026.

**Fast lane — attribution-independent, for creative decisions.** Retention curve shape, hold rate, CTR, comment sentiment. Free of attribution model and window, so usable within days. This is what iteration decisions run on.

**Slow lane — attributed, for scale decisions.** CPA, ROAS, incremental lift. **Every readout must state the attribution model and the window.** Meta ships two models that disagree by 24%, and the Insights default window is `["1d_view","28d_click"]` — so a 3-day read is structurally incomplete. Never compare a number across models or windows.

Never treat platform-reported CPA as ground truth. Never call a winner on 3 days of attributed data.

## 8. Non-negotiables

**One desire or problem, one avatar.** "People typically remember only one thing from your ad" `[T p.69]`. The most common failure the book diagnoses.

**Every word justifies itself in the sales process** `[T p.58]`.

**Simple beats clever, always.** "Clever copy might boost your ego, simple copy will boost your bank account" `[T p.72]`.

**You cannot create desire — only call it out and channel it onto the product** `[T p.25]`.

**Desire has a clock.** Before diagnosing creative, check the calendar and fulfilment. A Valentine's hook doing $70k/day collapsed to $1k/day seven days out because shipping could no longer arrive in time — not an ad problem `[T p.38]`.

**No invented statistics.** Ever, in any generated copy. The book itself models this badly ("9 out of 10 Facebook Ad experts…" `[T p.86]`) — do not follow it there.

**The ethics line.** The book opens with it and so does this plugin: everything here can be used to manipulate people; use it only for good `[T p.7]`.
