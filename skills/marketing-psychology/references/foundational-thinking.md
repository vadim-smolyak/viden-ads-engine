# Foundational thinking models (14)

Strategy and diagnosis. These decide *which* problem the creative is solving. Reach for them before writing, and when a run of tests has come back flat.

### First Principles
Break a problem down to basic truths and rebuild from there instead of copying what competitors do.
**In an ad:** stop rewording the category's standard claim and say the thing the product actually does that nobody in the category has said out loud.

### Jobs to Be Done
People do not buy products, they hire them to get a job done. The job is the outcome, not the feature set.
**In an ad:** lead on the job — the hole, not the drill. It also tells you who the real competitors are, including the indirect ones `[T p.10]`.

### Circle of Competence
Know what you actually understand and stay inside it, or bring in someone who does.
**In an ad:** do not write a technical mechanism you cannot explain in one sentence. Get the founder or the practitioner on camera instead.

### Inversion
Instead of asking how to succeed, ask what would guarantee failure, then avoid those things.
**In an ad:** before launch, list the five reasons this ad will fail — blurry audience, unproven desire, buried hook, mismatched visual, slow open — and kill each.

### Occam's Razor
The simplest explanation is usually the right one.
**In an ad:** when performance drops, check the obvious first — the page broke, the offer expired, stock ran out, the season turned `[T p.38]` — before diagnosing creative.

### Pareto Principle (80/20)
Roughly 80% of results come from 20% of inputs. Find the vital few.
**In an ad:** most accounts are carried by two or three messaging angles. Find them, then iterate and vary around them (canon §6) rather than spreading effort evenly.

### Local vs Global Optima
The best option nearby is not the best option available. Optimising the wrong thing well gets you stuck.
**In an ad:** a better hook on the wrong desire is a local optimum. Zoom out to the desire before polishing the line.

### Theory of Constraints
Every system has one bottleneck that sets throughput. Fix it before optimising anything else.
**In an ad:** if creative is producing cheap clicks and the page converts at half the category norm, more creative is wasted spend. Name the constraint before writing.

### Opportunity Cost
Every choice costs you what you did not choose.
**In an ad:** a concept slot spent on a fifth iteration of a known winner is a slot not spent opening a new market. Canon §6 sets when to stop iterating for exactly this reason.

### Law of Diminishing Returns
Past a point, more investment returns progressively less.
**In an ad:** the tenth variation of one message rarely earns what the third did. This is the quantitative version of the anti-staleness rule.

### Second-Order Thinking
Consider the effects of the effects, not just the immediate result.
**In an ad:** a discount hook lifts this week's revenue and teaches the audience to wait for the next discount. Ask what the ad trains people to expect.

### Map is not the Territory
A model or a dashboard represents reality; it is not reality.
**In an ad:** platform-reported CPA is a map (canon §7). So is the persona document. Stay close to real comments, reviews and support tickets.

### Probabilistic Thinking
Think in likelihoods, not certainties. Plan for the distribution, not the expected case.
**In an ad:** with a hit rate in the low single digits, no single creative is a plan. Judge the batch, and never bet a month on one concept.

### Barbell Strategy
Pair extreme safety with small, high-variance bets. Avoid the mediocre middle.
**In an ad:** hold most of the slate on proven messaging and reserve a fixed share for genuinely strange concepts. Moderate-risk creative produces moderate results, which is the worst outcome available.
