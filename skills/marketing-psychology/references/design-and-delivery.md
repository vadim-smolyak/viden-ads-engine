# Design and delivery models (10)

Behaviour design and friction. These act on the path around the ad — the page, the form, the funnel — and explain a large share of results the creative gets blamed for.

### Hick's Law
Decision time rises with the number and complexity of choices.
**In an ad:** one CTA, one destination, one variant. On the page, fewer fields and fewer paths. Every added option is added delay.

### AIDA Funnel
Attention, Interest, Desire, Action — the classic sequence.
**In an ad:** useful as a sanity check that all four beats exist, not as a script structure. The script structures are the five flows in canon §2, which are selected by awareness state rather than assumed.

### Rule of 7
Buyers need roughly seven touchpoints before acting. One ad rarely converts anyone cold.
**In an ad:** judge cold creative on its ability to earn the next touch, not the purchase. Treat the number as folklore-grade rather than a measured constant, and never quote it to a client as evidence.

### Nudge Theory / Choice Architecture
Small changes in how choices are presented move behaviour without removing options.
**In an ad:** ordering, defaults and emphasis do work that copy cannot. The recommended option, stated, is a nudge.

### BJ Fogg Behavior Model
Behaviour happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt coincide.
**In an ad:** the ad supplies motivation and the prompt. If ability is the gap — the process is hard, the setup is long — no amount of motivation in the hook fixes it. Show the ease instead.

### EAST Framework
Make the desired behaviour Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely.
**In an ad:** a four-point audit of an offer. Most underperforming offers fail Easy or Timely, not Attractive.

### COM-B Model
Behaviour needs Capability, Opportunity and Motivation together.
**In an ad:** Opportunity is the one creative usually ignores — is the moment right, is the product available, can it arrive in time. Desire has a clock (canon §8) is this model in practice.

### Activation Energy
The energy needed to start is separate from the energy needed to finish, and it is what blocks action.
**In an ad:** make the first step trivially small and say what it is. "Answer three questions" starts something; "get started" starts nothing.

### North Star Metric
One metric that best represents delivered value, used to align effort.
**In an ad:** decide before the test which number the creative is accountable to, and state the attribution model and window with it (canon §7). An unnamed target becomes whichever metric flatters the result.

### The Cobra Effect
Incentives that backfire and produce the opposite of the intent.
**In an ad:** optimising to a proxy produces creative that games the proxy. Hook-rate-maximising creative that sells nothing is the canonical example.
