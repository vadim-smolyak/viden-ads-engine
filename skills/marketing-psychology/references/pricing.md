# Pricing psychology (5)

How price is perceived. Mostly a page and offer concern, but the ad decides which number the reader sees first, so these matter to creative.

### Charm Pricing / Left-Digit Effect
Prices ending in 9 read as materially cheaper because the left digit dominates. $99 feels far below $100.
**In an ad:** use charm endings when the pitch is value. If the ad's whole argument is "cheaper than the alternative", the price on screen should be charm-priced.

### Rounded-Price (Fluency) Effect
Round numbers process faster and read as premium. $100 signals quality where $99 signals a deal.
**In an ad:** round the price when the pitch is craft, status or durability. A charm price undercuts a premium claim in the same frame.

### Rule of 100
Under $100, percentage discounts feel bigger. Over $100, absolute discounts feel bigger.
**In an ad:** on an $80 product say "20% off", not "$16 off". On a $500 product say "$100 off", not "20% off". Same saving, different perceived size.

### Price Relativity / Good-Better-Best
Price is judged against the options presented, not in absolute terms. A middle tier reads as reasonable between a cheap one and an expensive one.
**In an ad:** name the tier you want chosen rather than presenting the ladder. The ladder belongs on the page, where Paradox of Choice can be managed.

### Mental Accounting (pricing)
The same price framed differently lands differently, because people file costs into different mental accounts.
**In an ad:** "$1 a day" against a daily habit, not "$30 a month" against a rival subscription. Choose the account where your price looks small and the comparison is honest.
