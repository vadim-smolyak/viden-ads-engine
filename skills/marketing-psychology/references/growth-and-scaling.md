# Growth and scaling models (8)

Why what works at one spend level stops working at another, and what compounds. Reach for these when an account plateaus or a winner stops carrying it.

### Feedback Loops
Output becomes input. Positive loops accelerate, negative loops decay.
**In an ad:** winning creative funds more testing, which finds more winners. The loop breaks when the learning is not written down, which is why every test needs a logged hypothesis `[T p.95]`.

### Compounding
Small consistent gains accumulate; early gains matter most because they compound longest.
**In an ad:** a sustainable weekly cadence beats bursts. The book reaches the same conclusion from production reality — avoid churning out ads one week and nothing the next `[T p.97]`.

### Network Effects
The product gets more valuable as more people use it.
**In an ad:** where it exists, it is the strongest available proof, because the crowd is the feature rather than a testimonial about the feature.

### Flywheel Effect
Sustained effort builds momentum that eventually sustains itself. Hard to start, easier to keep.
**In an ad:** the research habit is the flywheel `[T p.90]`. The swipe file, the review mining and the comment reading get cheaper and more productive the longer they run.

### Switching Costs
Time, money, effort and data invested in the incumbent create retention.
**In an ad:** cut the switching cost in the copy for acquisition ("we import it for you"), and name it in retention messaging. Both are the same model pointed in opposite directions.

### Exploration vs Exploitation
Balance testing new things against optimising what already works.
**In an ad:** this is the formal statement of the iteration ladder in canon §6 — exploit a winner through iterations and variations, then explore. The ladder specifies where the switch happens.

### Critical Mass / Tipping Point
The threshold past which growth sustains itself.
**In an ad:** concentrate on one segment until it is genuinely won before broadening. Budget spread across five awareness stages usually wins none (canon §2).

### Survivorship Bias
Only the survivors are visible, so the failures never enter the analysis.
**In an ad:** the swipe file is all survivors. A competitor's long-running ad is evidence it was allowed to keep spending, not that its structure is transferable. Study your own losers too — the post-launch review asks what went wrong on winners *and* losers `[T p.88]`.
