# Visual formats — either medium (12)

Full entries for the formats named in the `visual-formats.md` selection table. Read that table first — it answers "which format" on its own. Each entry: what it is, awareness fit, what it is best for, the mechanic pairing, and any constraint or production note.

**Awareness shorthand:** `Un` unaware · `Prob` problem-aware · `Sol` solution-aware · `Prod` product-aware · `Most` most-aware · `Any` = a delivery vehicle carrying no stage signal (canon §2). **Mechanic pairings** name only mechanics defined in `creative-mechanics.md`; where no defined mechanic fits the field is absent — that is deliberate, and the mechanic then comes from the concept.

---

### AI Slop Animation
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** An AI-generated animated image or video producing a visual that would be cost-prohibitive or impossible to film — most often animating the product as a character or making an abstract mechanism visible.
**Awareness:** Un–Prob. A scroll-stopper for cold traffic.
**Best for:** Products where showing the invisible mechanism or ingredient is part of the sell; brands without production budget.
**Mechanic:** None inherent — the visual buys the stop, so take the mechanic from the concept.
**Note:** The one format where visible AI artefacts are the intended aesthetic rather than a defect. That exemption applies here only, and does not extend to AI avatars of humans — see the AI production path in `SKILL.md`.

### Before and After
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** Sequential or side-by-side contrast of the problem state and the solved state. The transformation must be shown together, not described.
**Awareness:** Prob–Prod. For people who know they have the problem and want proof this fixes it.
**Best for:** Skincare, cleaning, fitness, hair, home improvement — anywhere visible transformation is the promise.
**Mechanic:** Contrast Without Comment primary; Social Witness secondary.
**Constraint:** Restricted in health, weight-loss and skincare categories. Meta's policy text for health and wellness was never retrieved during research (`shared/evidence.md`) — name the risk, point to the policy URL, and get a human read. Do not clear it here.

### Comment Response
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** Creator replies to a surfaced on-screen social comment using the native reply feature. The comment is the frame.
**Awareness:** Prod–Most. Strongest as retargeting.
**Best for:** Brands with active comment sections and recurring objections. A native way to answer a hesitation.
**Mechanic:** Reframe, Trojan Horse — the comment sets up, the response sells.

### Grid Swap
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** A structured multi-cell grid where images, scenes or text alternate inside fixed uniform boxes. The layout stays constant.
**Awareness:** Un–Sol. The structure itself implies range, variety or comparison.
**Best for:** Multi-SKU brands, before/after sets, "three reasons why" concepts.
**Mechanic:** Implied Answer.

### Listicle
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** Numbered, bulleted or sequenced list of benefits, claims or tips.
**Awareness:** Sol–Prod. Delivers several points efficiently to someone in active consideration.
**Best for:** Products with three or more distinct benefits; comparison-heavy categories.
**Note:** Pairs naturally with the photo layout that calls out three benefits on the image `[T p.43]`.

### Meme
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** Uses an evergreen, culturally embedded meme template rather than a live trend.
**Awareness:** Un–Prob. Humour and cultural fluency earn the attention before the message.
**Best for:** Brands with a personality and a younger audience. Strongest when the product maps onto the template's existing meaning rather than being pasted into it.
**Mechanic:** Trojan Horse — the meme is the disguise, the message is the payload.

### Nostalgia
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** References or recreates a past era's aesthetic to trigger sentimentality.
**Awareness:** Un–Prob. Emotional pull first, product message second.
**Best for:** Audiences who grew up in a specific era; heritage products; brands whose story is old way against new way.
**Mechanic:** Implied Answer, Reframe.

### Pattern Interrupt
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** A surreal, absurd or unexpected image or video built purely to stop the scroll and open curiosity. Often AI-generated to reach implausible scenarios.
**Awareness:** Un. Cold traffic only. The goal is the stop, not the sell.
**Best for:** Saturated feeds where conventional creative disappears. Only works when the absurdity connects to the product's core promise — unconnected weirdness buys a stop and loses the audience immediately after.
**Note:** Canon §5 — this is a **format** here. "Pattern interrupt" also names a hook-layer psychological trigger. Always qualify which layer is meant. It is not a mechanic.

### Post It
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** A message written on a sticky note held to camera. The informality is the hook.
**Awareness:** Un–Prob.
**Best for:** Short, pointed messages — reminders, direct address, single-line urgency.
**Mechanic:** Trojan Horse — the form is disarming, so the message can be blunt.

### Sign
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** A person holding, or an image featuring, a sign, poster or whiteboard carrying a USP or CTA. Larger and more formal than a Post It.
**Awareness:** Any — depends entirely on what is written.
**Best for:** A single punchy claim. Protest-style creative, bold brand statements, offer callouts.
**Mechanic:** Implied Answer, Reframe.

### Unconventional Text Placement
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** Text in an unexpected physical location — on a person's back, a hand, clothing, a surface. The placement is the hook.
**Awareness:** Un–Prob. The oddness earns the stop.
**Best for:** Bold single messages that gain from visual disruption. Works when the placement is relevant to the message, not merely strange.
**Mechanic:** Implied Answer.

### Us vs. Them
**Medium:** Either — pick per canon §4 belief rule
**What it is:** Side-by-side or sequential comparison against a competitor or an alternative solution, with the comparison as the central element.
**Awareness:** Sol–Prod. Strongest while the prospect is actively weighing options. Also works earlier when the comparison is against the *old way* — this electrolyte against plain water — rather than a named brand.
**Best for:** Brands with a demonstrable advantage they win obviously and honestly.
**Mechanic:** Reframe when the alternative is named; **Borrowed Enemy** when it is described but not named — usually the better version.
**Constraint:** Naming competitors can trip Meta policy and many clients forbid it. Check brand constraints first. Prefer the unnamed version.
