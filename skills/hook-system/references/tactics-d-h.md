# Hook Tactics — Set A entries D–H

Full entries for the tactics named in `tactics.md`. Read the index first — it answers most lookups on its own. **Never read more than one shard for one hook set.**

**Awareness shorthand:** `Un` unaware · `Prob` problem-aware · `Sol` solution-aware · `Prod` product-aware · `Most` most-aware · `Any` = no stage signal (canon §2). **Triggers** are the eight in `triggers.md`.

---

## D–H

### Demographic Callout
**Is:** Names the audience segment outright so the right people self-select and the wrong ones drop. The segment can be a demographic, a **situation**, or a **history of what they have already tried** — the last is the highest-value variant, because "how many solutions have they already tried" is the question that drives everything upstream `[T p.25]`.
**Not:** Direct Address — that speaks to *you* without naming a segment.
**Fit:** Any. Mandatory in Special Ad Categories, where the copy is the only targeting instrument left (`shared/evidence.md`).
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out
**Ex:** *"For runners with bad knees."*
**Book variant** `[T p.38]`, pattern 13 — call out the person directly: *"To the person who has tried every Facebook ad strategy with no success."*

### Direct Address
**Is:** Speaks directly and personally to the viewer. Breaks the fourth wall.
**Not:** Demographic Callout — no segment is named, so it qualifies nobody.
**Fit:** Any. Strongest on social video, where the address is itself an interrupt.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out, Pattern Interrupt
**Ex:** *"If you're seeing this…"*
**Caution:** "you" framings that presume a diagnosis or a financial situation are a known policy-risk shape — see `shared/evidence.md`.

### Directive
**Is:** An imperative to change a behaviour, habit or mindset. Reframes how they act.
**Not:** Call To Action First — that instructs a purchase.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol. Health, finance, self-improvement.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
**Ex:** *"Stop wasting money on bras."*

### Exclusivity
**Is:** Signals that access is selective, limited or not for everyone. Desirability through gated entry.
**Not:** FOMO (missing a bandwagon); Bold Claim (the *claim* is singular, not the access).
**Fit:** Prod–Most. Premium, invite-only, high-ticket. Status-motivated buyers.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out, Urgency/Stakes
**Ex:** *"For serious lifters only."*

### Explainer
**Is:** Explains the *reason behind* something. "Why" framing that teaches a cause or mechanism.
**Not:** How To (steps); Listicle (numbered items).
**Fit:** Prob–Sol, where the audience feels the effect but does not know the cause. Science-led products.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Contrarian/Myth-Busting
**Ex:** *"Why your towels stay musty."*

### FOMO
**Is:** Anxiety about missing a trend, movement or social moment. Driven by belonging, not by a clock.
**Not:** Urgency (time or supply); Exclusivity (gated access).
**Fit:** Prod–Most. Trend-driven and socially visible products.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Urgency/Stakes
**Ex:** *"Don't be the last to try it."*

### How To
**Is:** An instructional promise — teaches the viewer to accomplish a task or fix a named problem. Includes the plain informational headline.
**Not:** Explainer (why); Listicle (items without instruction).
**Fit:** Prob–Sol, actively looking.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Pain Agitation
**Ex:** *"How to fix dry skin fast."*
**Book variant** `[T p.37]`, pattern 8 — offer information in the claim: *"How to scale Facebook ads in 2024."* Dating the claim raises relevance and shortens its shelf life; see `writing-process.md` on the calendar.

### Humor
**Is:** A comedic turn — incongruity, exaggeration, deadpan, or a self-aware logic loop — used as the stopping mechanism. The laugh is not decoration; it buys the next two seconds and lowers the guard the viewer brought to an ad.
**Not:** Relatability — that runs on recognition ("that's me"). Humor requires a *turn*: something has to be funny, not merely familiar. A recognisable scene with no joke is Relatability wearing a smile.
**Fit:** Un–Prob. Low-consideration, broad-appeal and socially shareable products. **Weak-to-harmful on high-anxiety pains** — medical, financial, fertility, debt — where a joke reads as dismissive of something the viewer is frightened of.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, then Identity Call-Out (an in-joke qualifies an audience faster than a description does)
**Ex:** *"The H in hydration stands for habit. Which is why there's no H in most people's morning routine."*
**The constraint that makes or breaks it:** the joke must be *about the product's truth*. A funny opener with no line back to the product is hook slop by another route — see `writing-process.md`. If the punchline could be lifted onto any brand, it is entertainment, not a hook.
**Provenance:** added in this plugin. `voice-patterns.md` Cluster 10 tags a template "Humor" and no source library ever defined it.
