# Psychological Triggers — the mechanism inside the frame

Eight triggers, and only eight. Canon §3's Mechanism layer: the tactic decides **what shape the claim takes**, the trigger decides **what fires in the reader's head**. A tactic with no trigger is a template; a trigger with no tactic is a feeling with nowhere to go.

Every hook runs at least one. The strongest usually run two — one to stop the scroll, one to make stopping feel deserved. **Across a set, vary the trigger.** Ten hooks on one trigger is one hook written ten times, and the set will fail together.

**The layer collision, per canon §5.** *Pattern Interrupt* is a trigger here **and** a visual format (indexed in `creative-concepting/references/visual-formats.md`, defined in `creative-concepting/references/formats-either.md`). They are unrelated: the trigger is a cognitive jolt in the opening line; the format is a production vessel. **Always qualify the layer** — "Pattern Interrupt trigger" or "Pattern Interrupt format", never the bare term. Same discipline applies to *Contrarian* (trigger here, tactic in the `tactics.md` index) and *Social Proof* (trigger here, tactic there): the trigger is the lever, the tactic is the frame, and a Social Proof tactic can perfectly well run on a Curiosity Gap trigger.

Awareness stages are canon §2's. Not restated here.

## 1. Pattern Interrupt
**Fires:** the reader's autopilot breaks. Something does not compute, so attention is spent to resolve it.
**Use when** the feed is saturated and the audience is scroll-numb, or the claim is ordinary and the framing has to carry it.
**Fit:** Any. **Tactics it executes:** Contrarian, Shocking Statement, Directive, Contrast, Reverse Psychology, Humor, Price Anchor.
- *"Stop moisturising your face."*
- *"Your dermatologist is making your acne worse."*

**Do not confuse interrupt with novelty.** Per `shared/evidence.md` (Kantar, 5 Feb 2026; Amplified/VCCP, 16 May 2025), what wins the first two seconds is a **recognisable** cue, not merely an arresting one. Interrupt the pattern with something they know, not something they cannot place.

## 2. Identity Call-Out
**Fires:** recognition. The reader concludes the ad is about them before deciding whether to care.
**Use when** the audience is niche, or a segment must self-select — and **mandatory in Special Ad Categories**, where copy is the only targeting instrument left (`shared/evidence.md`).
**Fit:** Any. **Tactics:** Demographic Callout, Direct Address, If Then, Exclusivity, Challenge, Limitation Removal.
- *"This is for the girl who cancels plans because of her skin."*
- *"If you're a bride with cystic acne, watch this."*

Specificity is the whole trigger. Narrower wording brings fewer stops and a far better audience of stoppers. Canon §1's corollary applies: naming them is not enough — give them a reason to stay `[T p.33]`.

## 3. Pain Agitation
**Fires:** felt recognition of a problem, sharply enough that scrolling past feels like avoidance.
**Use when** the audience is problem-aware and needs to feel understood before they will hear a claim.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol. **Tactics:** Relatability, Question, Directive, If Then, Warning, Limitation Removal.
- *"You've tried everything and your skin is still breaking out."*
- *"The worst part about cystic acne isn't how it looks. It's cancelling plans because of it."*

**Two limits.** State the pain once and move to the new reality — do not wallow `[T p.67–68]`. And do not imply knowledge of the reader's health or financial condition; that is a live policy-risk shape (`shared/evidence.md`).

## 4. Curiosity Gap
**Fires:** an open loop. The reader keeps watching to close it.
**Use when** you have no proof yet, no permission yet, and no relationship — the cheapest trigger to run and the easiest to run badly.
**Fit:** Any. **Tactics:** Curiosity, Explainer, Listicle, Storytelling, Question, Newness, How To.
- *"Nobody's talking about this Meta update."*
- *"The thing about clear skin that every skincare brand gets wrong."*

The loop must be closable by the ad. A gap the creative never closes reads as a bait and costs the click. Canon §1's third rule is this trigger's floor, not its ceiling.

## 5. Social Proof / Credibility
**Fires:** borrowed trust. Someone else already took the risk.
**Use when** the barrier is disbelief rather than desire — product-aware audiences that have not bought.
**Fit:** Prod–Most, and earlier when the proof is genuinely striking. **Tactics:** Social Proof, Authority, Statistic, Reasons Why, Confession, Risk Reversal, Claim Before/After.
- *"I've been breaking out since I was 12. Here's what finally worked at 29."*
- *"She cleared her skin in six weeks before her wedding. Here's exactly what she did."*

Specific and unpolished beats large and glossy. **Every number is the client's and verified — no invented statistics, ever** (canon §8).

## 6. Contrarian / Myth-Busting
**Fires:** the suspicion of having been misled. Reframes a belief they hold.
**Use when** research surfaced a real belief blocking the sale, or the category is full of repeated advice.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol. **Tactics:** Contrarian, Myth Busting, Belief, Explainer, Confession.
- *"Retinol is the reason your skin keeps purging."*
- *"The problem isn't your skin. It's what your doctor told you to do."*

Name a belief they actually hold, sourced from research (`market-research`). An invented myth reads as a straw man. When the belief is the whole barrier to purchase, the hook can only open the door — the dismantling happens in the body of the ad, and trying to finish it in one line produces a hook that argues instead of stopping.

## 7. Aspiration / Desire
**Fires:** the pull of the version of themselves on the other side.
**Use when** there is no acute problem to agitate, or the category is aesthetic, status-led or identity-led.
**Fit:** Un–Sol. **Tactics:** Aspirational, Belief, Claim Size, Claim Speed, Bold Claim.
- *"You're six weeks away from not thinking about your skin anymore."*
- *"What if your ads worked so well you could actually scale?"*

Aspiration only works on a desire that already exists — you cannot create desire, only channel it (canon §8) — and only on one that is **proven and large**, which is canon §1's first pre-hook gate and the reason the book's $1,000 ad failed with a technically perfect hook `[T p.65]`.

## 8. Urgency / Stakes
**Fires:** the cost of inaction. Waiting becomes the expensive option.
**Use when** the audience is convinced but idle, or a real deadline exists.
**Fit:** Prod–Most. **Tactics:** Urgency, FOMO, Call To Action First, Offer Only, Exclusivity, Warning.
- *"Every day you don't fix this, you're leaving money on the table."*
- *"Your wedding is in eight weeks. What are you doing about your skin?"*

Manufactured urgency is the fastest trust burn available: a price rise announced and not executed loses the customer permanently `[T p.83]`. And check the calendar before writing it — a genuine deadline can invalidate the whole offer when fulfilment cannot meet it (`writing-process.md`).

## Cheat sheet

| To… | Fire |
|---|---|
| Break autopilot | Pattern Interrupt |
| Make them self-select | Identity Call-Out |
| Make them feel understood | Pain Agitation |
| Open a loop | Curiosity Gap |
| Borrow trust fast | Social Proof/Credibility |
| Flip a belief they hold | Contrarian/Myth-Busting |
| Show what is possible | Aspiration/Desire |
| Make waiting expensive | Urgency/Stakes |

## Trigger by awareness stage — the default pairings

Stage comes from research, never preference (canon §2). These are starting points, not rules.

| Stage | Lead trigger | Support | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Relatable Pain Agitation or Curiosity Gap | Pattern Interrupt | Urgency, Social Proof — nothing to be urgent about yet |
| **Problem-aware** | Pain Agitation | Identity Call-Out, Curiosity Gap | Offer-led Urgency |
| **Solution-aware** | Contrarian/Myth-Busting | Pattern Interrupt, Social Proof | Pure Aspiration — they want differentiation |
| **Product-aware** | Social Proof/Credibility | Urgency/Stakes, Identity Call-Out | Curiosity Gap alone — they already know what it is |
| **Most-aware** | Urgency/Stakes | Social Proof | Pain Agitation — the pain is settled |

**A repair worth recording:** the source hook library carried its own five-stage table with the same stage names and different guidance from the rest of the stack. Deleted. Canon §2 is the single mapping — the stage selects the script flow as well as the trigger, which is the point of having one table.
