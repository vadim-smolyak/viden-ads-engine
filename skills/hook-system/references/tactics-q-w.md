# Hook Tactics — Set A entries Q–W

Full entries for the tactics named in `tactics.md`. Read the index first — it answers most lookups on its own. **Never read more than one shard for one hook set.**

**Awareness shorthand:** `Un` unaware · `Prob` problem-aware · `Sol` solution-aware · `Prod` product-aware · `Most` most-aware · `Any` = no stage signal (canon §2). **Triggers** are the eight in `triggers.md`.

---

## Q–S

### Question
**Is:** Poses the problem, challenge or gap in question form — ideally the sentence already running in their head.
**Fit:** Any, strongest at Prob.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Pain Agitation
**Ex:** *"Struggling with X?"*
**Book variant** `[T p.37]`, pattern 7 — state the claim as a question: *"Who else wants to scale Facebook ads with no hard work?"* "Who else" smuggles in social proof by presupposing that others already do.

### Reasons Why
**Is:** A number plus explicit "reasons why" phrasing. Credibility through structured rationale.
**Not:** Listicle — this requires the "reasons why" construction.
**Fit:** Sol–Prod, where logical justification precedes purchase.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Curiosity Gap
**Ex:** *"3 reasons why dermatologists love this."*

### Relatability
**Is:** Anchors in a shared everyday scene the audience recognises instantly. Empathy through familiarity.
**Not:** Humor — recognition is not a joke.
**Fit:** Un–Prob. Lifestyle and consumer products, broad reach.
**Trigger:** Pain Agitation, Identity Call-Out
**Ex:** *"When your coffee hits at 3pm…"*

### Reverse Psychology
**Is:** Tells the viewer *not* to act, to trigger reactance.
**Not:** Warning — that is sincere. This is not.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol. Ad-fatigued and contrarian personas.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Curiosity Gap
**Ex:** *"This isn't for you."*

### Risk Reversal
**Is:** Reduces the perceived cost of being wrong — guarantees, trials, safety nets.
**Not:** Limitation Removal — that removes a *capability* objection, this removes a *financial* one.
**Fit:** Prod–Most, stalled on hesitation. High-ticket or new-to-market.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Urgency/Stakes
**Ex:** *"Love it or your money back."*

### Shocking Statement
**Is:** A surprising, provocative or counter-intuitive claim that immediately unsettles an assumption.
**Not:** Contrarian (breaks a rule); Myth Busting (corrects a fact). Shocking Statement only needs to *jolt*.
**Fit:** Any. Built for saturated feeds.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Contrarian/Myth-Busting
**Ex:** *"You've been doing this wrong your whole life."*
**Caution:** the shock must belong to the product's story. Shock plus no connection is the textbook route into hook slop — `writing-process.md`.

### Social Proof
**Is:** Reviews, testimonials and popularity signals. Trust through volume or consensus.
**Not:** Authority — credentials, not headcount.
**Fit:** Prod–Most, hesitating.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility
**Ex:** *"300,000 sold."*
**Caution:** every number is the client's, verified. Never a placeholder that ships (canon §8).

### Statistic
**Is:** Quantified evidence — studies, surveys, measured results, usage data — as the credibility engine.
**Not:** Offer Only (a discount is not evidence); Claim Size (a promise is not evidence).
**Fit:** Sol–Prod. Analytical and sceptical audiences.
**Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Pattern Interrupt
**Ex:** *"9 out of 10 users…"* — **as a shape only.** No statistic ships without a source the client owns. The book itself models this badly at `[T p.86]`; do not follow it there (canon §8).

### Storytelling
**Is:** Drops in mid-moment on a personal or brand story. Narrative pull before any claim.
**Fit:** Any. Emotional products and transformations that need context.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Pain Agitation, Social Proof/Credibility
**Ex:** *"Three years ago I was broke…"*

## T–W

### Urgency
**Is:** Time or supply pressure that forces a decision now.
**Not:** FOMO (social belonging); Exclusivity (access).
**Fit:** Most. Sale windows and real deadlines.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes
**Ex:** *"Only 200 left."*
**Caution:** the deadline must be real. A stated price rise that does not happen costs trust permanently `[T p.83]`.

### Warning
**Is:** A sincere caution that halts the viewer's default behaviour until you explain why they should stop. Frequently aimed at the solution they are about to buy or already use.
**Not:** Reverse Psychology — that is a device; a Warning means it.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol, actively shopping and about to choose wrong.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
**Ex:** *"Don't buy another pillow until you see this."*
**Book variant** `[T p.38]`, pattern 12 — call out a solution or product they are currently using: *"Don't spend another dollar on Facebook ads until you watch this."* This is the sharpest Sol-stage frame in the library, because the failed prior solution is the thing they most want explained.
