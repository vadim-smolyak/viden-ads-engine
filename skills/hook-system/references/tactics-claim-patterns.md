# Set B — the five claim patterns with no Set A equivalent `[T p.37–38]`

These manipulate a claim rather than address an audience state. The 13-row mapping and an example shape for all 13 are in the index, `tactics.md`. The eight duplicates are pointers there; the five below are defined in full because no Set A tactic owns the move.

**Working method for all 13:** write the base claim in plain language first, then run it through the patterns. One claim yields a dozen hooks without a new idea, which is what makes this set the fastest volume engine in the skill (`writing-process.md`).

---

### Claim Size — "measure the size of the claim" `[T p.37]`
**Is:** Attaches a magnitude to the promise. The number *is* the hook.
**Not:** Statistic (evidence, not promise); Offer Only (a discount, not an outcome); Bold Claim (a superlative with no number).
**Fit:** Sol–Prod. **Trigger:** Aspiration/Desire, Pattern Interrupt.
**Ex (book):** *"Make an extra $10,000 a month with Facebook ads."*
**Constraint:** the number comes from the client's own results, or the pattern is unusable. Income and health-outcome magnitudes are a known policy-risk shape (`shared/evidence.md`).

### Claim Speed — "measure the speed of the claim" `[T p.37]`
**Is:** Attaches a timeframe to the result. Answers "how fast" before they ask.
**Not:** Urgency — that is a deadline on *their decision*; this is a clock on *the outcome*.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol, especially where prior solutions failed on how long they took.
**Trigger:** Aspiration/Desire, Curiosity Gap.
**Ex (book):** *"How to double your Facebook ads budget overnight."*
**Constraint:** substantiate the timeframe. It is the claim most likely to become a refund.

### Claim Before/After `[T p.37]`
**Is:** Contrasts one subject against its own earlier state. First-person transformation, stated as two numbers or two conditions.
**Not:** Contrast — that compares you to something *else*. This compares you to *you*. And not the **Before and After visual format** (`creative-concepting/references/formats-either.md`); that is the production vessel, this is the opening line's frame. Qualify the layer, the way canon §5 does for Pattern Interrupt.
**Fit:** Sol–Prod. **Trigger:** Social Proof/Credibility, Aspiration/Desire.
**Ex (book):** *"Before emotion baiting I was stuck at 1× ROAS. Now I regularly do 3×."*
**Constraint:** before/after *body* imagery is a known policy-risk shape (`shared/evidence.md`). The verbal frame is usually safe where the visual is not.

### Limitation Removal — "remove limitations from the claim" `[T p.37]`
**Is:** Names the disqualifier the reader is already using on themselves and dissolves it. "Even if…", "without…", "no X required".
**Not:** If Then (applies a condition); Risk Reversal (removes financial risk, not a capability doubt).
**Fit:** Prob–Sol, and outstanding for audiences who have tried and failed — the failure *is* the limitation they now believe in.
**Trigger:** Pain Agitation, Identity Call-Out.
**Ex (book):** *"How to win with Facebook ads even if you have no experience."*
**Where the limitation comes from:** research, never invention. `market-research` Levels 1–2 produce the real ones — reviews and comments are where people state what disqualifies them. The hook works only if the limitation is theirs.

### Newness — "stress the newness of the claim" `[T p.38]`
**Is:** Positions the claim as new — a new method, a new finding, a newly available thing.
**Not:** Bold Claim (singular, not new); Exclusivity (gated, not new); Curiosity (a loop, not a date).
**Fit:** Sol–Prod, and the natural frame when `positioning-mechanisms` has just named a new mechanism.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Pattern Interrupt.
**Ex (book):** *"NEW Facebook ads strategy doubles budget overnight."*
**Constraint:** novelty decays on the calendar and cannot be re-run indefinitely. Also note the 2026 correction in `shared/evidence.md` — **recognisable beats merely novel** in the first two seconds; "new" as a *claim* is fine, "new" as a *reason the viewer should stop* is weak.
