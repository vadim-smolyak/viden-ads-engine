# Hook Tactics — Set A entries I–P

Full entries for the tactics named in `tactics.md`. Read the index first — it answers most lookups on its own. **Never read more than one shard for one hook set.**

**Awareness shorthand:** `Un` unaware · `Prob` problem-aware · `Sol` solution-aware · `Prod` product-aware · `Most` most-aware · `Any` = no stage signal (canon §2). **Triggers** are the eight in `triggers.md`.

---

## I–P

### If Then
**Is:** Qualifies with a condition, then delivers the promise or instruction. Self-selecting and specific.
**Not:** Limitation Removal — that dissolves a disqualifier rather than applying one.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol, where the condition is nameable in the viewer's own words.
**Trigger:** Identity Call-Out, Pain Agitation
**Ex:** *"If you sit all day, try this."*

### Listicle
**Is:** Numbered or list framing that breaks information into items. No "reasons why" phrasing.
**Not:** Reasons Why (explicit phrasing); How To (steps).
**Fit:** Sol–Prod. Content-led and educational sets; audiences who respond to structure.
**Trigger:** Curiosity Gap, Social Proof/Credibility
**Ex:** *"5 steps to clearer skin."*

### Myth Busting
**Is:** Debunks a widely held misconception and corrects it. Names the belief, then breaks it.
**Not:** Contrarian — that defies accepted *advice*; this corrects a stated *error*.
**Fit:** Prob–Sol. Categories thick with misinformation; evidence-led brands.
**Trigger:** Contrarian/Myth-Busting, Pattern Interrupt
**Ex:** *"SPF 100 doesn't block 100%."*
**Book variant** `[T p.38]`, pattern 11 — challenge your prospect's beliefs: *"I thought Facebook ads was a scam until I saw this."* Two execution routes: first-person Confession phrasing (I held it, I was wrong) or third-person correction. When the belief is the real barrier to purchase, the hook names it and the script dismantles it — do not try to close it in one line.

### Offer Only
**Is:** The discount or monetary incentive as the entire hook. No urgency, gating or comparison attached.
**Not:** Urgency (time pressure); Statistic (proof, not price).
**Fit:** Most. Sale events, price-motivated segments.
**Trigger:** Urgency/Stakes
**Ex:** *"$500 off."*

### Price Anchor
**Is:** Frames cost against a familiar benchmark so the price feels smaller.
**Not:** Contrast — the comparison is to an everyday reference point, not to a rival.
**Fit:** Prod–Most. Mid-to-high ticket, or any product with known sticker shock.
**Trigger:** Pattern Interrupt, Pain Agitation
**Ex:** *"Less than your daily coffee."*
