# Buying motivations, angle types and micropersonas

Three sections. Read the angle-type section to choose the emotional territory, the motivations tables to name the driver, the micropersona section to get from a persona to a single voice.

---

## 1. The three angle types

Every messaging angle expresses one of three. The choice sets the emotional territory the hook will occupy.

| Angle type | Taps into | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain point** | Something frustrating, broken or hurting right now | Default for problem-aware audiences. Highest urgency |
| **Motivation** | A desire, aspiration or identity they are reaching toward | The product enables a positive transformation or a self-image |
| **Failed solution** | A previous attempt that let them down | Solution-aware audiences who have tried competitors. Builds the strongest contrast, and pairs directly with the hate-list method |

One angle can be expressed through all three. On a "glow for dull skin" angle:

- Pain: "My skin always looks tired and flat no matter what I do"
- Motivation: "I want people to tell me I look glowing without asking what I'm wearing"
- Failed solution: "Every highlighter I've tried just sits on top of my skin and looks cakey"

Choose by where the audience sits on the awareness ladder (`shared/canon.md` §2) and which territory has untested creative runway.

The failed-solution type needs the count of prior attempts and the reason each failed `[T p.25]`. Without that, it collapses into a generic pain angle.

## 2. The 20 buying motivations

Grounded in the Maslow lineage. Ten are core human needs; ten are product- and brand-level drivers. The distinction matters: a core need can carry an entire campaign, a product-level driver usually supports one.

Pick **one or two** per angle. More than two means the angle is not yet focused, which is the failure `[T p.69]` describes.

### Core human needs

| Motivation | What is actually driving the purchase |
|---|---|
| **Achievement** | Reaching a standard they set for themselves; visible progress; a personal record |
| **Autonomy** | Doing it themselves, on their own schedule, without depending on a professional or a partner |
| **Belonging** | Being part of a group that recognises them as one of its own |
| **Enjoyment** | Pleasure for its own sake — taste, comfort, fun, the good part of the day |
| **Esteem** | Being seen and respected by people whose opinion they care about |
| **Life event changes** | A wedding, a baby, a move, a diagnosis, a new job — a deadline the product has to meet |
| **Novelty** | Something new, first, or not yet seen by their circle |
| **Nurturance** | Caring for someone else — a child, a partner, a parent, a pet. The buyer is not the user |
| **Relief** | Making something stop. Pain, noise, worry, effort, mess |
| **Safety** | Reducing risk of harm, loss or embarrassment; protection for themselves or others |

### Product and brand-level motivators

| Motivation | What is actually driving the purchase |
|---|---|
| **After-sales service** | Confidence that a problem after purchase will be handled |
| **Availability** | Getting it now, in stock, in their size, delivered in time |
| **Brand mission** | Buying from someone whose stated purpose they want to support |
| **Brand reputation** | Trusting the name enough to stop comparing |
| **Financial savings** | Spending less than the alternative — the cheaper path to the same outcome |
| **Health benefits** | A measurable improvement to how their body or mind works |
| **Personalization** | It is made for, fitted to, or adjustable for them specifically |
| **Product features** | A concrete capability the alternatives do not have |
| **Quality** | It will last, and will not need replacing or apologising for |
| **Value for money** | Not the cheapest — the best ratio of what they get to what they pay |

### The check that matters

After picking, ask: **is this what is actually driving their decision, or what I wish were driving it?**

Confirm against research, not intuition. Trigger moments (VOC Bucket 2 in `market-research/references/voc-extraction.md`) name the motivation more reliably than anything else, because a trigger moment *is* a motivation firing.

Motivation notes for specific plays:

- **Nurturance** flips the persona — the buyer is not the user. The wall-art reposition to gifting `[T p.31]` is a nurturance reposition.
- **Life event changes** is the only motivation with a clock in it. It pairs with the seasonality doctrine (`shared/canon.md` §8) and needs a fulfilment check before launch.
- **Financial savings** on a premium product requires the expensive alternative to be understood *before* the price is introduced `[T p.79]`.
- **Belonging** is the engine of every identity play.

## 3. Micropersona refinement

A persona is a life context. A **micropersona is the specific mental state and internal voice inside that persona for this angle type.**

To get there: take persona + angle type and ask *"what is this specific person saying to themselves about this?"*

That internal monologue is the micropersona. It is what makes a hook feel written for one person.

Worked, on a single motivation angle for a complexion product:

| Persona | Micropersona | Internal voice |
|---|---|---|
| Service professional | "I want to feel put together even in scrubs" | "I may be in scrubs all day but I still want to feel good about how I look" |
| The confidence reclaimer | "I want to feel good in the mirror again" | "I stopped feeling like myself — I want that back" |
| The effortless one | "I want my skin to just look like this" | "I don't want to look made up, I want people to think I just woke up this way" |

Same persona group, same product, same angle type. Three completely different ads.

Write the internal voice in the customer's words, never the brand's. Where to find those words is `market-research`'s job — Levels 1, 4 and 5, plus the VOC standout-language bucket.

Stop at the micropersona. Turning that voice into an opening line is `hook-system`'s job.
