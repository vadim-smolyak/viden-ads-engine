# Cadence and fatigue — the two corrections, in full

Load this when the question is **how many creatives should we ship** or **is this creative fatigued** — including when a client or a lead asks about volume, or when a fatigue claim is about to cost a shoot. Nothing here is needed for a routine post-launch read.

`campaign-troubleshooting` points here for fatigue doctrine rather than re-deriving it. The iteration ladder itself is canon §6.

---

## Cadence — corrected

**The volume target is replaced by a quality gate.** The book's 6–12 new creatives/week `[T p.95]` is superseded.

- Motion *Creative Benchmarks 2026* (578,750 creatives, 6,015 brands, $1.29B, Meta only): top-quartile accounts by winner count launch **3.0/week against a 3.3/week median** — slightly *fewer* than the median. Hit rate **3.85%**. Roughly half of all ads never receive meaningful spend.
- State that benchmark whenever a client or a lead asks about volume, so expectations are calibrated. It cuts against Motion's own commercial interest, which strengthens it.

**The house rule:** cadence is whatever the team sustains **at quality**, gated on one written hypothesis per creative. This is the book agreeing with itself — `[T p.97]` already says consistency at quality beats volume, and warns against churning out ads one week and nothing the next. Never set a weekly number as the target; set the gate and let the number fall out.

## Fatigue — corrected

Two different things wear the same name. Separate them before anyone books a shoot.

| | **Message staleness** — real | **Delivery / auction effects** — not fatigue |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Same hooks, same messaging, nothing new to say `[T p.93]` | Rising CPM, rising frequency, cost drift |
| Evidence | The book's 2022 case: $200k/month account tanked after four months of different creatives carrying the same messaging. "When I investigate accounts that are underperforming 90% of the time, this is their main issue" `[T p.93]` | Meta's price per ad rose **+12% YoY in Q1 2026 and +12% YoY in Q2 2026** (29 Apr, 29 Jul 2026) — ~0.95%/month compounding, independent of any creative. Kantar (26 Apr 2024, ~20-year database, 1,582/1,645/1,706/1,292 ads across three markets): **no relationship between frequency and "fed up" scores**, and a *positive* correlation between frequency and Executional Branded Memorability |
| The check | Read the last 8 weeks of hooks and angles side by side. Is there a new idea in there? | Compare the account's CPM trend to its own history and to the platform drift. If it tracks drift, it is not fatigue |
| The fix | New concepts (canon §6) | Nothing creative. It's a bidding and margin conversation |

**Two hard requirements before declaring fatigue:**

1. **A concurrent control.** Engineering at Meta (5 Aug 2026): semantic content features are "especially helpful in cold-start scenarios" for new ads. A lift at swap time therefore proves nothing about the incumbent — without a control still running, you have measured cold-start, not decay.
2. **No fixed refresh intervals, ever.** "Refresh every N weeks" fires on auction inflation, not creative quality. ARF (23 Sep 2025): true creative wearout is less common than once assumed, requiring heavy short-term frequency *and* poor creative.

Diagnose *message staleness*. Never write "fatigue" without saying which of the two you mean.
