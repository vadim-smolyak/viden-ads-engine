# The five script flows

Source: `[T p.47–49]`. The awareness-stage → flow mapping is canon §2 — this file is the execution detail.

**Selection rule:** the flow is chosen by what research says the market has already tried `[T p.25, p.33]`, never by preference. Get that wrong and no amount of rewriting saves the script.

---

## 1. Symptom → Problem → Solution → Product → Offer

**For unaware markets.** "This is the most complex script you can write and is not our usual, but it has its place when we're opening up unaware markets" `[T p.47]`.

Use when the customer feels *something* but hasn't named it, and doesn't know a solution category exists. The most expensive flow to execute — you're paying to educate — so only reach for it when the awareness stage genuinely demands it, or when a testing hypothesis is specifically about opening an unaware market `[T p.96]`.

The book's worked example — attic insulation `[T p.47]`:

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| Symptom | "High energy bills" |
| Problem | AC running all day uses a lot of energy |
| Solution | Insulating your attic space to retain cool air |
| Product | Our insulation service — best price, best customer testimonials |
| Offer | "Beat the heat today and get a quote" / "save 60% off today if you click the link below" |

**Where this flow fails:** dwelling on the symptom. The book's $100 ad "spent too much time discussing the problem… this video dragged it on and made it boring" `[T p.65]`. Name the symptom, confirm it, move.

---

## 2. Problem → Solution → Product → Offer

**For problem-aware markets.** The book's framing: symptoms are what you feel; the *problem* is the diagnosis. "When you're sick, you have symptoms of an illness but you have no clue what you have. Then you go to the doctor and get a diagnosis. This diagnosis is the problem you have" `[T p.48]`.

Worked example `[T p.48]`:

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | "How to get rid of a sinus infection in 48 hours" |
| Solution | "All you need to do is take a zpack" |
| Product | Our zpack — what's good about it, how it works, testimonials |
| Offer | "Beat your sinus infection today" / "save 60% off today" |

**The move that makes this flow work:** name the problem more sharply than the customer can name it themselves. That's what earns the rest of the ad.

---

## 3. Solution → Product → Offer

**For solution-aware markets.** No symptom, no problem — a *desire for a solution* `[T p.48]`. The book's example is weight loss: the market already knows what it wants.

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| Solution / desire | "How I got ripped and toned for the beach this summer" |
| Product | "All I had to use was Nick's training program" — then how good it was, the results, and any objections |
| Offer | "Start losing weight today" / "save 60% off today" |

**Attach a mechanism here.** A solution-aware customer has tried solutions before, so the answer to "why will this one work?" is the mechanism — see `positioning-mechanisms`.

---

## 4. Product → Offer

**For product-aware markets.** They've seen the product, aren't sold on the brand yet, and are actively comparing competitors. "These scripts we want to write are more focused on increasing trust with our brand, proving superiority of a better product in the marketplace, and answering any questions or objections consumers have" `[T p.48]`.

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| Product | Open on a testimonial from someone raving about results, plus a few smaller testimonial clips |
| Offer | "Start losing weight today" / "save 60% off today" |

**Lead with proof, not introduction.** Introducing a product to someone who already knows it wastes the only attention you have. Objection work belongs here — hand to `belief-objection-engineering`.

---

## 5. Offer

**For most-aware markets.** Rare. "It's rare that we create ads around this, but we will for major sales events" `[T p.49]`.

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| Offer | Call out the offer, say why they need to act now (about to sell out), say where to act. The end. |

Urgency devices belong here — limited time, price increase, limited inventory, reward for buying now. All four, with their ethics constraints, are in `belief-objection-engineering` `[T p.83]`.

---

## Diagnosing the wrong flow

Choosing the wrong flow produces symptoms that look like writing problems but aren't:

| Symptom | Likely flow error |
|---|---|
| Ad feels preachy or lecturing | Symptom-first flow used on a problem-aware or solution-aware market — you're educating people who already know |
| Ad feels abrupt, nobody converts | Solution-first or Product-first used on an unaware market — you skipped the setup they needed |
| Strong engagement, no purchases | Right flow, missing offer or missing mechanism |
| Works for a week then dies | Not a flow problem. See `campaign-troubleshooting`. |
| High CTR, high bounce | Flow is fine; the landing page doesn't continue the thought `[T p.93]` |

**Budget note.** A limited budget concentrated on one awareness stage beats the same budget spread across five. Pick the stage where the volume of addressable desire is largest, which is a research output, not a guess.
