# Script diagnosis — the spend-tier case studies

Source: `[T p.65–66]`. The most useful diagnostic material in the book, because all four ads were real and the failures are *specific*.

Testing premise: "all ads are given a chance to utilize the full budget. This means that the better ads will rise to the top and spend more, while the ads that are bad will spend less" `[T p.65]`.

---

## The four ads

### $100 — failed at the hook

Hook: *"Everyone talks about how amazing being retired is."*

| Golden rule | Result |
|---|---|
| Calls out ideal audience | ✓ |
| Implies a benefit | ✗ |
| Drives curiosity | ✗ |

Plus two script-level failures `[T p.65]`: "the script spent too much time discussing the problem. You need to discuss the problem to connect to the solution but this video dragged it on and made it boring." And the video was slow-paced.

**Lesson:** two of three golden rules missing is fatal before the script matters. And even a necessary problem beat can be overrun — connect and move.

### $1,000 — perfect hook, wrong desire

Hook: *"How to romanticize your life this summer."* All three golden rules passed. Visuals good, pacing good.

The failure was upstream of everything: **"we're going after a desire for 'romanticizing your life,' which I have never heard of personally… if it's not a proven desire across the marketplace or large enough, it will not scale"** `[T p.65]`.

**Lesson — the most important one in this file.** A flawless hook on an unproven desire cannot scale. Validate the desire *before* writing the hook. This is why canon §1 puts desire validation as a pre-hook gate.

### $10,000 — audience blur plus hook/visual mismatch

Hook: *"Create an intimate & memorable date night this weekend."* All three golden rules passed. Visuals good, pacing good.

Two failures `[T p.66]`:
1. **Audience not clear enough.** "People who are looking to have a date this weekend" is a real audience but not a sharp one. The book's own fix: go after *guys who want to impress their girl this weekend* — "that would have likely been better + more clear."
2. **Hook/visual mismatch.** "We also show a girl by herself in the beginning of the video when the hook is said, that does not look like a memorable date night."

**Lesson:** the opening visual must *depict* what the hook promises. A hook that passes on paper still fails if frame one contradicts it. Check this specifically in pass 6.

### $100,000 — everything aligned

Hook: *"This is for the girlies who want a girl's night soon."* All three golden rules, clear audience, good visuals, good pacing. "Everything about this video was perfect and is why it scaled" `[T p.66]`.

Worth noting where this hook came from: two women tagging each other in an ad's comments saying "Girls night soon?" `[T p.22]`. The best-performing hook in the book was lifted from customer language found in Level 1 research — not invented.

## The diagnostic ladder

Read a failing script in this order. Stop at the first failure — fixing a lower item while an upper one is broken wastes the reshoot.

| # | Check | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Is the desire proven and large?** | Stop. No script fixes this. Back to `market-research`. |
| 2 | **Is the audience unambiguous?** | Sharpen the avatar, then rewrite the hook. |
| 3 | **Does the hook pass all three golden rules?** | `hook-system`. |
| 4 | **Does frame one depict what the hook promises?** | Pass 6, and possibly a reshoot of the opening. |
| 5 | **One desire, one avatar throughout?** | Focus problem — `craft-heuristics.md`. |
| 6 | **Right flow for the awareness stage?** | `script-flows.md` — check the wrong-flow symptom table. |
| 7 | **Is the problem beat overrunning?** | Pass 4. |
| 8 | **Is belief established, or only asserted?** | Pass 2 — reach for the logical moves (5, 6, 7, 8). |
| 9 | **Does it read aloud like a person?** | Pass 5. |
| 10 | **Are the visual directions shootable?** | Pass 6. |

If all ten pass and the ad still underperforms, it is not a script problem. Go to `campaign-troubleshooting` — and note its cost-ordering rule, because most "creative problems" turn out to be fulfilment, calendar, page or positioning problems.

## The sag point

A weak second half is **not** grounds for scrapping a script.

Kantar (29 Jul 2024, 40 ads, ARF Attention Validation Initiative) found **a product introduction at second 7 created new attention.** Attention is re-winnable mid-ad.

Diagnosing a sag:
1. Find the line where interest drops — usually where the script stops delivering new information or stops advancing the story.
2. Ask what *new* thing arrives there. If nothing, that's the sag.
3. Fix with a pass 2 move that hasn't been used yet, or by cutting the preceding lines so the next beat arrives sooner.

Do not fix a sag by adding a second desire. That trades a sag for a focus failure, which is worse.

## Failure patterns not covered by the ladder

| Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Strong hook, strong script, no purchases | Missing offer, missing mechanism, or a page that doesn't continue the thought `[T p.93]` |
| Worked for a week, then died | Message staleness or an auction effect — not a script problem. `campaign-troubleshooting`. |
| Every script from the team sounds the same | The team is re-hooking the same content. Canon §6 — variations need new footage `[T p.86]`. |
| Reads well, tests badly, repeatedly | Suspect the desire again. Return to item 1. |
| High engagement, wrong customers | Audience qualifier missing from the first line. Sharpen it in the hook, not the body. |
