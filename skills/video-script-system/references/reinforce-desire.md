# Pass 2 — The 15 ways to reinforce desire

Source: `[T p.52–56]`.

**Why this pass exists.** People don't believe a claim the first time. "It's only believed after numerous examples are shown" `[T p.52]` — the book's analogy is how political or religious belief actually forms: not on first hearing, but after repeated exposure over time. So the job of this pass is to show the product satisfying the desire from *several* angles, not one.

**The rule that stops this pass going wrong: "Our goal is not to use all 15 but to layer them in as they fit"** `[T p.56]`. Three or four well-chosen moves beat fifteen crammed in. A script that uses all fifteen has lost focus, which breaks a more important rule `[T p.69]`.

---

## The 15 moves

| # | Move | What it does | The book's example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Show it achieving the desire** | The single most direct demonstration | Guy walks into a bar in a new shirt, girls run up to him |
| 2 | **Show it doing so again and again** | Converts a one-off into reliability | Waking up full of energy every day for 30 days |
| 3 | **Where** the desire gets satisfied | Anchors it in a physical place the viewer can picture | Focus on the location where it happens |
| 4 | **When** the desire gets satisfied | Puts it in their actual daily timeline | "Every morning when they wake up" |
| 5 | **How** it works | The logical mechanism — this is the belief-builder | "Just take one pill each morning and it will increase your red blood cells, making you feel full of energy" |
| 6 | **How to test the claim** | Hands the viewer a verification they can run themselves | "Next time you spill red wine on the carpet, one swipe of Zap Away and it's all removed" |
| 7 | **Experts and customers commenting** | Borrowed credibility on the specific desire | Named quote + credential |
| 8 | **Superiority vs the competitor** at satisfying it | Comparative, on the dimension they care about | "Other cars take 3 seconds to 60, the all-new C8 Corvette takes only 2.8" |
| 9 | **Metaphor** for the desire | Gets past literal-mindedness; also handles unshowable products | The book notes industries using everyday objects to stand in for what can't be shown |
| 10 | **Speed** of satisfaction | Compresses time-to-value | "Only 60 seconds after taking this and I can feel a strong buzz" |
| 11 | **Size** of satisfaction | Quantifies the payoff | "Emotion baiting will add an extra $10,000 per month to your Facebook ads" |
| 12 | **Sensory** — feel, smell, taste | The most under-used move on this list | "Tastes like a freshly picked strawberry from the sunny fields of California" |
| 13 | **The benefits of the desire being satisfied** | Second-order payoff — where the real emotion usually lives | "You'll make so much money that every time you go out with friends you can cover the bill with no worries" |
| 14 | **Dramatise how hard it is without your product** | Makes the alternative feel exhausting | "Unless you want to buy a Honda Civic, spend thousands tuning it, have it in the shop for months, and then only come close to how fast the C8 is" |
| 15 | **Problems they'll face because the desire got satisfied** | Counter-intuitive and memorable — a "problem" that is really a brag | "You'll make so much money you'll have a line of people banging on your door for handouts" |

---

## Choosing which to layer

Pick by what the script is missing, not by going down the list.

| The script's weakness | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Claim feels asserted, not proven | 5 (how), 6 (test the claim), 7 (experts) |
| Feels like a one-off fluke | 2 (again and again) |
| Emotionally flat | 12 (sensory), 13 (benefit of the benefit), 1 (show it) |
| Reader is comparing you to something | 8 (superiority), 14 (dramatise the alternative) |
| Payoff feels small or vague | 10 (speed), 11 (size) |
| Product can't be shown directly | 9 (metaphor), 3 (where), 4 (when) |
| Too earnest, no personality | 15 (problems from success) |
| Reader can't picture it in their life | 3 (where), 4 (when) |

**Balance check.** Moves 5, 6, 7 and 8 are logical. Moves 1, 12, 13 and 15 are emotional. Alternate them rather than clustering `[T p.67]`.

## Worked layering

The book's running script before pass 2 `[T p.51]`:

> Using Emotion-Baiting to scale Facebook Ads past $100 per day Fast
> I've tried a lot of other Facebook ad strategies to scale
> But nothing has come close to how fast I can scale with Emotion-Baiting
> This account was literally just at $100 daily a week ago
> And now we're spending $1,000/day on ads while maintaining profitably
> If you want to learn more about emotion-baiting, there is a free training below

After pass 2, two moves layered in `[T p.56]` — note how few:

> …
> **My inventory ran out in a week after applying this.** ← move 15, problems from success
> **You can keep burning time and money on outdated Ad Strategies.** ← move 14, dramatise the alternative
> …

The author's note on this: some moves "were already used in the beginning script, as these just come naturally to me now" `[T p.56]`. Audit the draft for moves already present before adding — that's what keeps the count low.

**Then expect pass 4 to cut some of this.** In the book's own progression, both lines added here are later removed `[T p.58]`. Layering generously and cutting hard is the method.
