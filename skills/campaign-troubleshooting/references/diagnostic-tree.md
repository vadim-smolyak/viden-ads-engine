# The six causes in full `[T p.92–94]`

Each cause: the symptom shape, the check (with its cost), the fix, and the book's case. Work in the cost order stated in `SKILL.md` — a cause lower in that order is not eliminated just because a higher one is interesting.

---

## 1. No urgency in the desire `[T p.92]`

**Symptom shape.** Craft is good, hooks clear the golden rules, nothing gets traction. Or the account works at low spend and will not scale — the desire exists but too few people want it solved *this week*.

**The check** (moderate — a reading exercise, not a data pull). Take the desire the ad names and ask: what happens to this person if they do nothing for another month? If the answer is "nothing much", there is no urgency.

**The book's urgency comparison.** "Snoring" versus **"not being able to sleep in the same bed as my wife because of my snoring."** Same product, same buyer. The second is more painful to solve *now* — it has a cost that is being paid daily, and a relationship attached to it.

**The wall-art case.** "Put something unique on your wall" had no urgency and the account stalled. The replacement — **"When was the last time you surprised your sweetheart?"** — created urgency by making people realise it had been a while, and the account went back to $10k+ days. Note the mechanism: the hook did not invent a desire, it made an existing neglect *felt*. Canon §8 — you cannot create desire, only call it out and channel it.

**The fix.** Re-angle onto a consequence that is already being paid. Not a bigger promise — a nearer one. Route to `creative-concepting` with the consequence named, or to `market-research` if you cannot name one from evidence.

**What this is not.** Urgency in the *desire* is not urgency in the *offer*. A countdown timer on a desire nobody feels is still no urgency.

## 2. Ads too educational `[T p.92]`

**Symptom shape.** Hook rate healthy, hold rate collapses partway, few clicks convert. The ad is informative and inert.

**The check** (cheap — watch the ad). Where does it stop making them want and start teaching? Timestamp it. People scroll for dopamine; over-educating turns them away.

**The fix.** Focus on the desired state, and **use curiosity to push the technical detail onto the landing page** `[T p.92]`. Then confirm the page actually carries that detail — a curiosity gap deferred twice is a broken promise, which becomes cause 5.

**The exception — when the spec *is* the desire.** A better iPhone camera. Buyers of some categories want the specification because the specification is the outcome they are shopping for. Education is correct there. Test whether your buyer is comparing specs or comparing feelings before cutting the detail.

**Route.** `video-script-system` for the cut, `hook-system` if the curiosity gap needs rebuilding.

## 3. Seasonality — and desire has a clock `[T p.92, p.38]`

**Symptom shape.** Two very different shapes, so distinguish them:

| Shape | Reading |
|---|---|
| Gradual drift over weeks, ads unchanged | Season shape. The state the ad speaks to is no longer the state they're in |
| Collapse within days, no creative change | **A clock ran out.** Almost always fulfilment or a date, not creative |

**The check — always first, always free.** Shipping cutoff dates. Stock. Promo end date. Holiday calendar. Last year's same weeks. Nothing here costs anything to check, and it explains more sudden collapses than every creative theory combined.

**The Valentine's case — the one to remember.** A Valentine's hook running at **$70k/day collapsed to $1k/day seven days out** `[T p.38]`. The cause: shipping could no longer arrive in time. The desire was intact, the ad was intact, the creative was blameless. **A fulfilment problem misread as a creative one.** Anyone who reshot in that week burned a week.

**The hoodie collapse.** Seasonal category, season ended, performance went with it. No creative fix exists for a season that is over.

**The Rose Skin Co. counter-move.** They **re-position every summer** — girls by the pool in bikinis — to match the state people are actually in. The lesson is not "expect a summer dip", it is that seasonality is a *positioning* input you can plan for, twice a year, in advance.

**The fix.** Operations first (extend the cutoff, change the shipping promise, change the offer's date framing). Then re-position to the current state — do not reshoot last season's angle in this season's weather.

## 4. Repeating the same strategy `[T p.93]`

**Symptom shape.** Slow decay over weeks or months, not days. Plenty of new creatives shipping. Each one feels fine in isolation.

**The book's case.** A 2022 client at **$200k/month** with winners found. Performance tanked **four months later**. The cause was not volume and not craft: different creatives carrying **the same hooks and very similar messaging**. Nothing felt new to the market.

> **"When I investigate accounts that are underperforming 90% of the time, this is their main issue"** `[T p.93]`.

Weight this cause accordingly. If the account has been running eight weeks or more, check it early even though the fix is expensive.

**The check** (moderate — free, but it takes an hour). Pull the last 8 weeks of hooks as verbatim text and read them in one column. Then read the angles. Ask one question: **is there a new idea in here?** If every hook is a rephrasing, you have found it. The roadmap matrix in `testing-and-iteration` makes this visible without the manual pull.

**The fix.** New concepts, per canon §6's ladder. Not more iterations, not more re-hooks of the same footage. If the account has been sitting on iterations and variations of one winner for two months, the ladder has been exhausted and nobody noticed.

**The distinction that matters.** This is **message staleness**, not "fatigue". Rising CPM and frequency are auction effects, not evidence of this cause — the fatigue doctrine and the concurrent-control requirement live in `testing-and-iteration`. Do not declare this cause from a cost chart; declare it from reading the hooks.

## 5. Landing page `[T p.93]`

**Symptom shape.** CTR healthy or improving while CPA rises. Checkout initiations flat against rising clicks. A winner that died on a date with no creative change.

**The book's scope.** "Landing page" covers product pages, home pages, collection pages, advertorials, listicles and sales pages. Two failures:

1. The page **does not follow through on the thoughts the ad opened.**
2. The page **does not share the ad's theme.**

The standard: *"Your prospect should never click on your ad and be like, 'What the hell did I end up at?'"*

**Division of labour — do not do both jobs here.**

| Job | Owner |
|---|---|
| Auditing ad → page continuity and theme, and writing the corrected page lines | `ad-copy-qa` (post-click alignment) |
| Scoring the page as a conversion instrument, finding the killers, the CWV gate | `references/page-audit-rubric.md` in this skill |

Run the gate and the score here. When the finding is a continuity break, hand the rewrite over rather than drafting it.

**The check** (cheap — one page load, one CWV read, one score). Start with Core Web Vitals at mobile p75. A failing page invalidates the creative read entirely, so there is no point scoring anything else first.

**The fix.** Page work, in the order the rubric ranks it. Never re-hook a page problem — a better ad sends more traffic into the same leak.

## 6. Positioning `[T p.94]`

**Symptom shape.** Every new creative lands flat despite strong hooks, good craft and a real desire. Iterations do nothing. Concepts do nothing. The account feels like it is arguing with the market.

**The cause.** How you perceive the product and how prospects perceive it do not line up.

**The check** (moderate). Ask what the product *is* to the buyer — the category they file it under, the alternative they compare it against, and what they think it costs them to switch. Compare that to how the brand describes itself. The gap is the diagnosis.

**The fix.** Hand off to **`positioning-mechanisms`**. Do not attempt a repositioning inside a troubleshooting run — it changes the angle, the awareness stage, the script flow and the visuals, which is a full concepting cycle, not a fix.

**Diagnostic value.** Cause 6 is the most expensive to act on and the easiest to reach for prematurely. Only conclude positioning after causes 1–5 have been checked and named as ruled out. "It's a positioning problem" with nothing ruled out is a way of saying you did not diagnose.

---

## Reporting a diagnosis that comes back empty

Sometimes all six are clean and performance still moved. Say that. The honest answers are: auction drift (see the CPM note in `SKILL.md`), a measurement change rather than a performance change (canon §7 — check whether the attribution model or window changed under you), a tracking break, or a competitor's promotion. An empty diagnosis with the calendar, the page, the hooks and the positioning all ruled out is a genuine finding and stops a pointless shoot.
