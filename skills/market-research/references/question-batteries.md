# Question batteries — the 5 levels, verbatim scope

One section per level. Read only the section for the level being run. The closing section is the synthesis battery — read it when moving from findings to personas.

Every answer gets a line on the tally sheet, and a `*` for every repeat sighting `[T p.15]`.

---

## Level 1 — Your own assets `[T p.14–15]`

Skip this level entirely if the brand is pre-launch.

### 1a. Winning ads

- What hook grabbed attention?
- Who are we appealing to?
- What are we promising?

### 1b. Comments on winning ads

- What are people saying?
- What specific use cases do they name?
- What reasons do they give for wanting to buy?
- What problems have they struggled with?
- What reasons do they give for *not* buying?
- If they tagged a friend, did they say why that person needed it?

The last question is the highest-yield line in this battery. Read every tag thread.

### 1c. Instagram tagged posts

- What are people saying?
- *How* are people talking — sentence shapes, not just topics?
- What slang and category language recurs?
- Where is the content created?
- What filming and editing style do they use?
- Apparent age, gender, location?

### 1d. Reviews on the website

- What is good?
- What is bad?
- Who are people buying for?
- Who are these people?
- What did they struggle with before?
- What goals did the product help them hit?

Read 1-, 2-, 3-, 4- and 5-star for a representative mix `[T p.15]`. A 3-star review usually states the real objection more plainly than a 1-star.

For structured extraction of this material, switch to `references/voc-extraction.md`.

### 1e. Support inbox

- What are the common questions?
- What are the common complaints?

## Level 2 — Direct competitors `[T p.16]`

Direct competitor = selling the same product.

Find them by searching the product category on Google, Amazon and YouTube. Then pull their ads through Foreplay (routing table in SKILL.md) rather than browsing manually.

### 2a. The ads battery — 10 questions

1. Which ads have run the longest?
2. What hooks are they using?
3. What type of people are shown?
4. Where is it filmed?
5. What key things are shown that people want?
6. What key problems are shown as solved?
7. Where does the traffic go — landing page, product page, advertorial, or home page?
8. Photo or video? Professional or UGC?
9. What key visuals recur across their set?
10. Who is the target persona?

Question 1 is the prioritisation question. In Foreplay, answer it with `order="longest_running"` or `running_duration_min_days`, and treat longevity as a spend proxy only — it is not performance data.

Question 7 is answerable from the `link_url` field without leaving the tool.

### 2b. The reviews battery

Run the six Level 1d questions against the competitor's own site reviews.

If the competitor's site has no reviews, find the same product on Amazon and use those `[T p.16]`.

## Level 3 — Indirect competitors `[T p.17]`

Indirect competitor = satisfying the same desire with a different product. Rose Skin Co.'s indirect set was shaving, professional laser appointments, waxing and Nair `[T p.10]`.

Find them from the top-ranked Google listings and the top YouTube videos on the *topic* — not the product.

Run both Level 2 batteries unchanged: the 10-question ads battery and the reviews battery.

In Foreplay, query the desire rather than the product name. "Hair-free skin at home" surfaces a different indirect set than "IPL device".

Why this level pays: "We get a lot of good ad inspiration from understanding how indirect competitors are selling to the same desire or problem" `[T p.17]`. The enumeration of what people hate about each of these solutions is the raw material for the superior-product perception — that method lives in `positioning-mechanisms`.

## Level 4 — Content around the desire or problem `[T p.18]`

Pretend you have the problem. Search the way the buyer would, in the buyer's words.

Places: YouTube, TikTok, Quora, Reddit, Google.

- What are the standard solutions for this problem or desire?
- What type of people are in these videos?
- What hooks, titles and thumbnails are used?
- **What do the comments say?** "Some of the best gold can be found here" `[T p.18]`

Read the comments before the content. The content is one creator's framing; the comments are the market's.

## Level 5 — General niche content `[T p.19–20]`

Not problem-specific. Whole-niche trends — gym content, car content, clothing content.

Two purposes:

1. **Learn what content works in the niche**, so ads can replicate the trend and not look salesy.
2. **Find the benefit behind the benefit.** More money → travel? → a Lambo?

- What content formats and structures are winning in this niche right now?
- **What makes these people happy?**
- **What makes these people sad?** `[T p.19]`

The happy/sad pair is the point of the level. Worked: entrepreneur niche → buying nice things; gym niche → sadness at missing PRs. The derived hook was "buying my first Rolex at 27" `[T p.20]`.

## Synthesis battery — findings to personas `[T p.21–22]`

Run these seven against the tally sheet, once per persona:

1. Who is my target audience?
2. What is their problem?
3. What is their desire, want or goal?
4. What have they tried before?
5. How old are they?
6. Who are they?
7. What do they look like?

Question 4 is load-bearing. The count of solutions already tried "drastically changes what we must say in an ad" `[T p.25]` and it is what selects the awareness stage, and therefore the script flow (`shared/canon.md` §2).

Output shape, per persona: a named prose paragraph, then two to four hooks in the persona's own language, then one line of visual direction per hook. Persona count is whatever the research produced `[T p.22]`.
