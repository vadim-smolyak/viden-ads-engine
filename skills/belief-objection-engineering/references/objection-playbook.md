# Objection Playbook — lookup table

Find the objection in the prospect's own words, take the move, use the precedent. Rows marked **pattern only** have no case in the doctrine source; execute them, but do not present them as a proven precedent.

Objections are **found, not guessed** — the words in column one should come from reviews, comments, the support inbox or the frequency tally, via `market-research`. An objection nobody actually raises is a line of copy spent on nothing.

## Price and value

| They are thinking | Diagnosis | The move | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | Too expensive | Establish the cost of a **more** expensive solution first, then introduce yours. Sequence is the whole move — see `SKILL.md` | Rose Skin Co.: thousands in laser appointments, then one $200 payment `[T p.79]` |
| "I can do this myself for free." | Too expensive, against an indirect competitor | Cost the DIY route fully — money, time, repetition, indignity. Enumerate what people hate about every existing solution and let the product's benefits land on those hatreds | Rose Skin Co.'s indirect set was shaving, waxing, Nair and laser appointments; razor burn, weekly time, razor cost and awkward shower positions became the claim "30 mins a week, from home, pain-free, zero ingrown hairs" `[T p.10–11]` |
| "I'll wait for a sale." | No reason to act now | A named limited-time offer or a transparent price increase announced the day before. Both must be real | `[T p.83]` |
| "Cheaper options exist." | Superiority, not price | Win on the one dimension the market already cares about. Do not add dimensions to justify the gap | Apple ships only screen size, battery life and camera quality each year — *"that is all people desire"* `[T p.28]` |

## Indifference and urgency

| They are thinking | Diagnosis | The move | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I don't need this." | Not important enough | Extend one step to the benefit of the benefit, and show the verifiable proxy on screen | Attic insulation → cooler house → **lower electric bills**, with visuals of the lower bill `[T p.80]` |
| "Yeah, one day." | No urgency in the desire | Restate the problem at its most painful concrete consequence rather than its category name | "Snoring" versus *"not being able to sleep in the same bed as my wife because of my snoring"* `[T p.92]` |
| "It's a nice-to-have." | No urgency; possibly the wrong desire | Re-aim the same product at a desire that already carries urgency, rather than arguing harder for this one | Wall art stuck at $30k/mo on "this will make your wall look great" → *"When was the last time you surprised your sweetheart?"* → past $300k/mo, and the ideal customer changed with it `[T p.31]` |
| "Maybe after [event]." | Calendar, not creative | Check fulfilment and season before touching the ad. Desire has a clock | A Valentine's hook doing $70k/day collapsed to $1k/day seven days out because shipping could no longer arrive in time `[T p.38]` |

| They are thinking | Diagnosis | The move | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I don't understand what it does." | Too complicated | Simplify the mechanism to one everyday action. Pass the restatement test in `SKILL.md` | PayPal: "sending currency over IP addresses" → **"you can send money over email"** `[T p.80]` |
| "It looks like a hassle to use." | Too complicated, at the protocol level | Show the entire use end to end in real time. Simplify the protocol, not the description of it | **Pattern only** |
| "There was too much detail, I switched off." | Over-education | Hold the desired state on screen and push the technical detail to the landing page on curiosity. Exception: educate where the desire *is* the spec | `[T p.92]`; the spec exception `[T p.92]` |
| "I had to work out what they meant." | Clever copy | Rewrite plainly. *"Clever copy might boost your ego, simple copy will boost your bank account"* | `[T p.72]`, canon §8 |

## Disbelief

| They are thinking | Diagnosis | The move | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I don't believe the claim." | Belief, proof-shaped | Authority — a person the market already credits. Or hand the prospect a test they can run themselves | Authority `[T p.82]`; the self-test — *"next time you spill red wine on the carpet, one swipe of Zap Away"* `[T p.53–55]` |
| "Sounds too good to be true." | Belief, overreach-shaped | Do not raise the claim. Dramatise how hard the outcome is **without** the product, and cut one link off the benefit chain | The Honda Civic tuned for months just to approach a C8 `[T p.53–55]`; over-extension caution `[T p.80]` |
| "Prove it actually holds up." | Belief, durability-shaped | Escalate the demonstration once the messaging is already winning — common use first, then extreme | Flex Seal moved from a leaking window seal to drilling a hole in a boat and floating it `[T p.87]` |
| "Everyone claims that." | Belief of parity | Run the four-step below. Qualify by their current solution before you claim anything | The insurance case `[T p.81]` |

## Category-level belief

| They are thinking | Diagnosis | The move | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| "These are all a scam." | Category belief | Make the belief the hook, then tell the story of one person who held it and was wrong | `[T p.82]` |
| "The word alone puts me off." | Belief attached to the name | Rename — only where the new name is true | "All courses are scams" → creators sold **"systems"** `[T p.81]` |
| "It won't work for someone like me." | Belief, identity-shaped | Social proof: several people who held that exact objection and were wrong. Cast to the "looks like" archetype | `[T p.82]`; casting in `creative-concepting` |
| "I tried something like this and it failed." | Failed desire — the desire survives, the hope does not | Do not re-argue the desire. Explain **why the previous solution failed**, then show a mechanism that removes that specific failure | Lost hope and the need to re-instil it `[T p.25]`; the anti-snoring mouthpiece — hundreds of competitor reviews showed failure through discomfort, so the client's version was positioned as the most comfortable and wearable for all eight hours `[T p.95–96]` |

## The four-step template, generalised

Derived from the insurance case `[T p.81]`. Use it whenever the objection is parity — "this is the same as what I already have".

1. **Qualify by their current solution.** Name what they already use, explicitly, so only those people lean in. Not the demographic — the solution.
2. **Destabilise it with one specific, verifiable fact** about that solution that costs them something they care about. One fact. Specific enough to be checked.
3. **Show the emotional consequence of not having known** — people upset they did not find out sooner. This is the step teams skip, and it is the step that converts a fact into a motive.
4. **Then introduce the product** as the resolution.

The product cannot appear before step 3. If it does, step 2 reads as a sales device rather than a warning, and the structure collapses.

**Before using it:** the fact in step 2 must be substantiable by the client. Canon §8 — no invented statistics, and that includes plausible-sounding facts about a competitor's terms. In health, financial and political categories this shape carries real policy exposure — see the policy gate in `SKILL.md` and `shared/evidence.md`.

## The per-line audit

Run on a finished script before it goes to `prelaunch-qa`.

Read line by line and at each line ask *"how would my prospect handle that?"* `[T p.81]`. Mark every line that would draw a raised eyebrow, a "sure", or a silent "prove it". Then, for each mark, decide one of three things: resolve it on that line, cut the line, or move the claim behind proof. Do not defer it to a later line — the objection forms where it forms.

Two failure modes to watch while auditing:

- **Wallowing in the problem.** State the pain once and move to the transformation. The book's diagnosed loser dwelt on the problem and got boring — the $100 ad `[T p.65]`. Sell the new reality against the current situation `[T p.67–68]`.
- **Answering an objection nobody raised.** Every answered objection costs attention. If it is not in the research at frequency, cut it — canon §8, every word justifies itself in the sales process `[T p.58]`.

## Pre-ship gate

All seven, before the objection layer is signed off:

1. Every objection answered appears in the research, with a frequency tally behind it.
2. Price objections establish the more expensive alternative **before** the price.
3. The benefit chain stops at the last link the prospect can verify from their own life.
4. The mechanism survives the restatement test with someone outside the category.
5. Every scarcity, inventory and price-rise claim is true and operationally enforced — the order form is actually capped.
6. No fabricated number anywhere, including competitor terms and buyer counts.
7. Anything in a health, financial, or political category is flagged for a human policy read, with the specific policy URL named.
