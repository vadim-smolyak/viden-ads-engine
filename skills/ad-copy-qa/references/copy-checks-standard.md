# Copy checks — standard checks 8–14, the CTA doctrine, and the pre-Pass gate

Deep-path shard. The seven priority checks are in `copy-checks.md`; the policy gallery in `copy-policy-risk.md`; the before/after examples in `copy-worked-examples.md`.

---

## Standard checks

### 8. One idea
One desire or problem, one avatar (canon §8). Count the desires the copy names. More than one is the most common failure there is.

### 9. Use case present
The copy names a real moment: the commute, the airport, the third night of bad sleep, the Sunday reset. Product description with no moment attached does not give the reader a place to put the product.

### 10. CTA earns the click
See the CTA doctrine below.

### 11. Surface fit
Every surface inside its limit, hook ahead of the truncation point. See `platform-limits.md`. Flag anything over and supply a trimmed alternative that keeps the hook intact rather than trimming from the front.

### 12. No repetition across the batch
Read the batch together, not ad by ad. If three ads open the same way or reuse a phrase, flag the second and third. Reused phrasing is a documented review reject and it also collapses the batch into one test.

### 13. Proof is real
Quotes verbatim from the artefact's VOC bank. Never fabricate a quote. Never tidy a real one into brand voice — the roughness is the proof. Attribution matches what the artefact records; do not upgrade a customer into an expert.

### 14. Scarcity and urgency are true
Stated once and calm. "Only a few made" beats a countdown. Then check it is actually true: stock, deadline, price change, seasonal window. Desire has a clock (canon §8) — a hook that depends on arrival before a date is a fulfilment claim as well as a copy line, so check the calendar before the copy.

## The CTA doctrine `[T p.76]`

The most-neglected element in the ad. **An effective CTA reminds people why they should click, or gives them a compelling reason to act now.**

Don't → Do:

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "Click to buy now" | "Click below to get smooth, hair-free skin" |
| "Click to buy now" | "Click below to take advantage of our back-to-school special" |
| "Click to buy now" | "Click below to save 30% on our mushroom elixir" |
| "Click below to get a quote" | "Click below to get protected today!" |

The pattern: the verb stays, the object becomes **the thing the reader came for** — the outcome, the named offer, the saving, the protection. A CTA that names the transaction ("buy", "sign up", "submit", "learn more") describes what the advertiser gets. A CTA that names the outcome describes what the reader gets.

Two further rules:
- **Name the offer after something relevant to the audience** when the ad is offer-led — a named special ("back-to-school") reads as more relevant and more urgent than a bare percentage `[T p.83]`.
- **Split the CTA into two beats** when the read is crowded: what exists, then how to get it. "There is a step-by-step training." / "You can access it by clicking the link below." `[T p.59]`

## Final gate before a Pass

- [ ] Every line grammatical and natural, corrected lines shown for any that were not.
- [ ] No empty claims, no hollow superlatives.
- [ ] Every number traces to the brand artefact or a client-supplied figure.
- [ ] Copy matches the product, variant, colour and scene shown.
- [ ] Hook clears all three golden rules (canon §1).
- [ ] Simple over clever throughout.
- [ ] Voice, banned words and punctuation match the brand artefact.
- [ ] One desire, one avatar.
- [ ] A real use case is named.
- [ ] CTA names the outcome, not the transaction.
- [ ] Every surface inside its limit, hook ahead of truncation.
- [ ] No phrase reused across the batch.
- [ ] Quotes verbatim from the VOC bank.
- [ ] Scarcity stated once, calm, and true.
- [ ] Policy risks flagged with URL and a human-read instruction, and nothing described as compliant.

All boxes checked: Pass. Otherwise: Needs changes, each issue with the line, the rule, and the rewrite.
